DECODING AN INFLUENCER

Gordon Ramsay: from fierce TV chef to multi-platform teacher.

How a recognisable persona, useful content and smart collaborations help one chef speak to more than one audience.

The starting point: a powerful brand persona

Ramsay’s well-known television persona — demanding, direct and highly skilled — gives his content instant recognition. That distinct voice helps people identify a Gordon Ramsay clip in a crowded feed. But his digital presence also shows a more approachable side: a chef who teaches techniques, reacts to food trends and shares family-friendly moments.

Strategy 01 — make expertise useful

On YouTube, Ramsay turns professional knowledge into accessible, repeatable value. Videos such as Cooking Classics with Gordon Ramsay package cooking guidance around familiar dishes and clear demonstrations. This is content marketing: the viewer receives practical value first, while Ramsay’s credibility and wider food brand stay top of mind.

His content does not only say “I am an expert”; it lets viewers experience that expertise in their own kitchens.

Strategy 02 — borrow attention through collaborations

Ramsay regularly appears with celebrities, athletes and online creators. These collaborations use audience overlap: a guest introduces Ramsay to people who already follow them, while Ramsay offers the guest his food expertise and entertainment value. It is a practical example of cross-promotion, making the content more discoverable than a standard recipe video.

AUDIENCE SHIFT

Who is he talking to now?

His original audience was largely TV viewers who came for restaurant drama and elite-chef authority. Today, his ecosystem reaches home cooks, food fans, aspiring chefs, short-form viewers and families. The official channel’s 9 million subscriber milestone has since grown to roughly 22 million followers on YouTube, while his linked Instagram and TikTok accounts create additional entry points.

Strategy 03 — expand without losing the core

Ramsay expands horizontally across platforms and formats. Long YouTube videos serve viewers wanting instruction or entertainment; fast, trend-aware clips suit social feeds; premium cooking classes and restaurant experiences provide deeper engagement. This is a multi-channel, funnel-style strategy: entertaining content builds reach, educational content builds trust, and experiences such as masterclasses offer a route to action.

The key lesson is consistency. Although the tone changes by platform, the promise stays recognisable: high standards, food knowledge and a memorable personality.

MARKETING TAKEAWAY

Influence grows when expertise is packaged for different audiences — and when collaboration makes that expertise visible to new communities.

ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IN ONLINE MARKETING

Food domination with AI.

Woolworths, personalisation and the changing Australian grocery experience.

Introduction:

Artificial intelligence is continuously changing the business landscape and how we interact with customers online. In the online marketing space, AI can personalise the customer's shopping experience by learning what they actually like and, with the help of algorithms, predict what they will like now or in the future. For an existing e-commerce business, this can prove to be profitable and provide an easier shopping experience for other customers.

An Australian retailer that has been using AI actively to engage with its customers is Woolworths. Woolworths has developed a significant online shopping presence through the use of AI and Olive (Woolworths' shopping assistant). The website’s use of AI has been increasingly visible through personalised shopping experiences and predictive shopping experiences. Woolworths' agenda on using AI is to understand the customer much better and understand how to proceed further(through the use of marketing campaigns and providing better products).

This blog will specifically examine Woolworths and its use of AI in the online marketing space, focusing on personalisation and consumer behaviour. We will also go through a comparison against its local competitor, such as Coles too look at how they are doing in comparison to Woolworths.

How Woolworths Uses AI in Digital Marketing

The main application or tactic Woolworths uses to understand its customers better is personalisation. Woolworths uses algorithms or deep-learning algorithms to understand the customer better and learn what they actually like and what they are drawn to. For example, the Woolworths application now uses the existing information about the person through their phone and sends them offers on products they may actually like.

This can seem very different from mass marketing, as it makes the marketing experience more personal, giving special attention to every person. This sometimes makes us wonder how a website can learn so much about us. But the truth is, Woolworths uses machine learning and deep learning algorithms to understand a consumer/customers behaviour a lot better.

A particularly interesting example is the use of “smart basket”. This feature uses predictive AI to analyse what the customer has already bought and what they are likely to purchase again. Customers can review the items that are suggested and remove the already existing item that is recommended by the AI assistant. (Woolworths, n.d.).

There is also significant relevance that Woolworths’ digital platform has become very important in revenue generation. Woolworths has reported a weekly traffic of 31 million people visiting the website in the 3rd quarter of 2026, which is a surprising increase from the past year by a whopping 10%.

However, it is important to understand that not every online shopping experience is directly caused by the AI assistant. It can be influenced by many other factors, such as prices and seasonal promotions. The use of AI or an AI assistant for Woolworths has proven to be effective, but we have yet to understand its contribution to customer loyalty and long-term effects carefully.

Woolworths vs Coles: Who Is Leading?

Coles has also invested heavily in artificial intelligence. They report that they use AI not just to understand their customers but also has intergrated AI powered search as well. They have partnered with OpenAI to understand and explore how a personalised shopping experience can get better.

Coles has also experimented with a smart trolley which isn’t officially out in Australia. It has sensors to track the items, track spending and help you with checkout.

This comparison only goes to suggest that Woolworths has big competition in the AI space, given that Coles has invested heavily in OpenAI.

However, Woolworths is comparatively ahead of its time, making AI more accessible and visible to its existing customers (with the help of Smart Basket and Olive). As compared to coles, using more AI in the back-end rather than the front-end. Woolworths is putting AI directly in front of customers through its app. This makes AI part of product discovery, meal planning and basket building.

Ethical Issues: When Personalisation Goes Too Far

Despite the benefits, AI does have its disadvantages. Let's talk!

The first main issue with the use of AI in Woolworths is privacy. Woolworths claims that it uses customers' personal information to provide a personalised customer experience. This can seem like a valuable experience, but it’s questionable how much data they are using to understand a person.

The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission (ACCC) has speculated that the data that has been shared with the company is not absolutely transparent, and the choice regarding data collection is not completely understood by the customer. As time goes by, the ACCC has also noted that the importance of data is likely to increase as artificial intelligence becomes more widely used (ACCC, 2024).

There can also be some concerns around sensitive information; Olive may also use information that is shared during conversations with customers. Which preference dietary exceptions. Although this can improve the usefulness of the service, such information requires careful handling because it may reveal sensitive aspects of a customer's life.

Conclusion

Woolworths shows that AI is an important part of the online marketing space in the Australian retail market. With the help of personalised marketing, Olive A! and the Smart Basket shopping assistant, Woolworths uses AI to reduce shopping action, improve product exploration and create a more memorable customer experience.

However, AI also creates challenges. The more Woolworths relies on customer data to predict behaviour and personalise marketing, the more important privacy, transparency and consumer control become. The comparison with Coles also shows that AI is becoming a competitive requirement rather than a unique advantage in Australian grocery retail.

Overall, Woolworths looks to be using AI quite well, particularly because its technology is highly integrated into the customer experience. However, its long-term success will not only depend on how intelligent its tech is, but also on how it uses customer data responsibly. The use of AI-driven grocery marketing should therefore not simply be about predicting what customers will buy next, but about helping customers make better choices while maintaining their trust and control.
